Creativity : Q&A

Some of the questions posed on PollEv during our Q&A, with answers.

We gave you the opportunity to ask anonymous questions on Poll Everywhere.

You can add more questions on Poll Everywhere and follow up on any of these answers in the Discussion Forum.

Does our GitHub have to have data depiction on it or are we there to show the progress of our data collection and store that in GitHub?
 

The GitHub is to show that you are making progress with the project, so the commits could be data collection, commits or commits that involve some coding efforts... We just want to see that you can use GitHub and that you are engaging with your repository consistently and over time.

In essence, it doesn't matter what the commits are, we just need to see that you're committing!

At what point should we start drawing the sketch on Processing?
 

One of the things that we tried to show in the Creativity : Core materials, and the creativity lecture is that the best approach is to start small, experiment incrementally and develop ideas as you are sketching. So I would expect you to be making graphics in Processing right from the outset and linking small developing data sets to your sketches to depict your data very soon.

Don't see this as a big task. Start simple and draw a couple of shapes that are sized or colored according to a couple of data items in a small data file... Just as we do in the lecture notes and examples.

Can I cook while recording my data?
 

I don't expect a full risk assessment before each observation is made and recorded, but do be sure to be safe and not put yourself or others at risk when collecting data. Taking photos while cycling, taking photos of hazard lines in the middle of roads while traffic is passing, climbing large trees to collect the tallest leaf of each impressive Platanus hispanica that you pass, are examples of risky data collection that you should avoid.

So please be sensible adults and be sure to act with caution and safety in this task, and life generally.

In our final sketch, do we need interaction?
 

The grading criteria do not mention interaction, so there is no absolute need. However, Processing is designed for interactive sketches, and interaction is a great way to be expressive and creative ... and we do ask you to be expressive and creative.

Given that you are here to learn, and there is such opportunity for interaction, and that interaction is associated with originality, I would expect people to want to enjoy exploring creative interactive potential.

Is the method that is going to be assessed in the Dear Data Part 1 check-in, the methods that we have made or will a prompt be given to us to make ourselves?
 

You will be given a prompt and asked to write a method.

So ensure that you know the structure and syntax of methods, and of course, some of the basics of Processing. The best way to prepare for this is to work through the examples in the materials that we've provided on Moodle and on the Dear Data 100 task.

For the submission for Part 1, on my screenshot should it show the 25 data points or all of my data?
 

You need to show that you have 25 data points. So make sure that whoever is assessing your submission can clearly see that you have made 25 observations and recorded these somehow in 25 data 'points' or entries.

Some of the images in Data : Core and Data : Collection show what this might look like.

Are we supposed to know what each other is doing? What happens if you end up doing the same topic as someone else somehow?
 

Being original is a challenge if you don't know what currently exists: acknowledged. But the SCAMPER and ESCAPE approaches give you so many opportunities for originality in data collection and depiction that solutions with high levels of similarity are highly unlikely.

Topics can be broad, but the specifics of a thoughtfully considered and creatively constructed, Dear Data 100 project make it extremely likely that it will be original. If you fear that your work may not be original, then use the creativity techniques to make it more unique.

Can we choose either ESCAPE or SCAMPER and not lose marks?
 

We don't really work in a way in which marks are lost... They are gained. For Part 2 we ask students to "describe & justify [their creativity] with reference to SCAMPER terminology". So being able to describe the way in which you used SCAMPER or the ESCAPE derivative, and the particular techniques and their consequences, is what we are after here.

Simply use the language of these creativity methods, and convince your assessor that you have used them to influence your approach.

Can I recreate an artwork using the data instead of a graph?
 

Personally, I see large amounts of overlap between artwork and graphs.

So as long as your art contains data... I think it's a graph. And if your graph is artistic, I think it's probably an interesting graph. So... Yes! Data art.

Is it fine to display the data in multiple ways?
 

It is, and this is where creativity and interaction and originality can come in.

If you decide that you want to show data in multiple ways from multiple perspectives or with alternative encodings / depictions, then to me that is being expressive and original in your project.

My idea is something I have not seen before as I had the idea so how would I explain it with SCAMPER or ESCAPE?
 

Well, the thing to do here would be to make a change... Reverse something, or modify something, or amend something... And see where you get. If you don't like the results, then you can explain that your design decisions involved testing your approach with SCAMPER or ESCAPE, and you found that these creativity techniques did not result in any improvement in terms of the extent to which you are happy with your design.

I'd be very surprised if this was the case, but if it is, then you need to use the SCAMPER or ESCAPE terminology to explain your creative efforts and decision.

Do we need to have a sketch for Part 1 or just data?
 

Just the things we listed: the data, and some commits. The commits may be data commits, or they may be code commits, and the code may or may not be related to your sketch. See above.

The more I explain this the more I fear that I'm making something that is simple more complicated. No sketch for Part 1.

For the Part 1 of the Dear Data project, are we meant to fully complete the data visualisation for the data that I've collected? Or can I just complete that for Part 2?
 

See above.

Part 2 is where (and when) we need the sketch.

Do we strictly need to use code to show the data, or can we also use images?
 

The first four words of assessment requirements are: "Create a Processing sketch". So you do need to do that. You can load images in the sketch, see the Botanical Icons in Creativity : Core and I would encourage you to be creative in you're thinking about how you might use images.

The Images : Core materials offer plenty of ideas, and the Processing documentation offers even more.

Is it fine if my Dear Data sketch looks like an Excel graph? (Bar graph for example)
 

Well, we are looking for originality... so by definition if your sketch looks like something you've seen before then it is not entirely original.

As such, I would encourage you to use the SCAMPER or ESCAPE methods to be a little more creative and expressive in the way in which you depict your data.

How many commits do I have to do?
 

There is no "have to". But for Part 1 to get the most marks, you need three commits that are spaced over a period of more than two weeks. That gets you 25 marks.

Of course, it's probably an idea to make many more commits, and doing so will help you learn and develop something interesting that you're pleased with. But it won't directly give you marks at this stage. But the exercise is not all about marks at this stage... learning to use GitHub effectively will be useful to you throughout your university education and beyond.

Is it OK if I create my graphs as images and then load the images using the sketch like PowerPoint?
 

I can think of ways in which this could be expressive, but I also think there's a danger that what do produce could be very routine and that this approach might not involve you engaging with the kind of learning and coding that we're trying to encourage and support you into. Processing, and the features that we've introduced, are designed to explicitly support people introducing exactly the kind of thing we're asking for here... which is exactly the kind of activity and output that employers are asking of Computer Science graduates.

So while I can feel positive about creative approaches to problem-solving (?) I worry a little if people are not entering into the spirit of the activity. And if you end up making graphs in excel and loading an image into Processing, you're not learning much, and you're not going to impress the assessors. This would feel like a totally wasted opportunity for learning at university.

Which brings me to a final statement! 🏂
 

You are investing a lot of money and a lot of time, a large proportion of your life, in a Computer Science degree that, if you are successful, will have effects on and contribute to an even larger part of your life.
We are trying to equip you with ideas, capabilities, and learning opportunities that will help you become effective, competitive and professional Computer Scientists. So we encourage you to embrace all the opportunities available and experiment and aim high. We do not recommend efforts that do not embrace the potential of the technologies that we're using ... even if they enable you to gain marks.

Some of you will remember me showing up at induction with a snowboard... and trying to persuade you that this was a metaphor for learning. We give you the equipment, and you practice, with a few tumbles, but eventually, with a bit of help from us, master the equipment and technology that we're providing for you so that you can go places. You will need to do this to succeed in a phenomenally, competitive and bewilderingly dynamic job market. Now, while I welcome aall of the questions that have been asked here, and really appreciate the efforts of those who've put them forward, some of the questions suggest that people may be asking whether, rather than sliding down the nursery slopes strapped to a snowboard, they can instead descend the hill by walking down and carrying their board under their arm. Well, doing that is not going to help you get very far. You won't be able to navigate the big mountains that are coming. You need to get on the snowboard, even if you topple over a few times. These are safe slopes with soft snow, and they are great places to develop your technique.
