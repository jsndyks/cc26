Dear Data

This term the #CreativeCoding activity will help you learn about some key programming concepts and use them to do some data visualization. We hope that you will learn a little about yourself, your new colleagues, the world you live in and Computer Science through this process.

#CreativeCoding focuses on the Processing environment for coding, and riffs off the Dear Data Project to help you think creatively and learn about seeing and interpreting the world through data.

This session introduces the Dear Data Project and six initial tasks that will provide a focus for our #CreativeCoding this term.

Please complete them before the end of Week 01.

Dear Data : Part 1 - Their Project

1. Postcards

ever since we were young, we have been fascinated with collecting and organizing information from the world around us.

We are going to ask you to collect data this term.
Data about yourselves.
Or some aspect of the world that interests you.
And we are going to broadly follow a model introduced in the Dear Data Project.

Just over 10 years ago, two amazing creative women - one in London, one in New York - embarked upon a creative data sharing experiment. The idea was to get to know each other (creatively) through their data.

Giorgia & Stefanie collected personal data about a different theme each week over the course of a year.
That's 52 themes for data collection: ◻️◻️◼️◼️◻️◼️◻️◻️

Each week they explored the agreed theme by depicting aspects of their data on a postcard in visual form.
They then posted the hand-drawn cards to each other on opposite sides of The Atlantic.

This is all nicely summarised in the short Dear Data (Big Bang Data) video that we you need to watch.

Please watch the short Dear Data (Big Bang Data) video.

2. The Book

The visualizations produced in the project and reflections upon what they, and the process throiugh which they were produced, revealed were published online and in a book Dear Data.

It emphasises the notion of

data as a starting point for questioning and understanding the world around us,
rather than data as the definitive answer to all of our questions.

Dear Data is entertaining, revealing and inspiring enough that we want to use some of its ideas in learning about ourselves through data - learning about the relationships between code, data, ourselves and each other.

Creative Material

We are excited by the approach that Giorgia and Stefanie have taken.

They ...

see data as creative material like paint or paper

... that can be a starting point for play and expression as we begin to find ourselves.
They encourage us to use data and its depiction as a new way of seeing and engaging with the world.

We will try to use the #CreativeCoding activities to equip and inspire you to do the same.

3. 52 Weeks

We want you to look a the WebSite and the Book before the end of the week.

On the website you can find the 52 topics, one for each week, with designs by both of the authors and a reflective commentary on each: By Week — Dear Data.

We would like you to choose THREE of these and study them in detail:

  • Spend time looking carefully at the graphics
  • Consider the key - what is shown and how?
  • Try to detect any patterns and trends - can you see any? If so, what do these suggest
  • How do the two representations of the theme (from Stefanie and Giorgia) differ? How are they similar?
    In terms of :
    • what was collected?
    • how it is graphically depicted?
    • what is, and isn't, revealed?

We hope that you will all study three very different examples - try using this Number Picker Wheel to choose your three weeks (remember to set 'MAX' to 52).

Task 3 : 3/52 Weeks [SPIN & STUDY]

Go to the By Week — Dear Data page and select three examples to study in detail.

  1. What aspects of the phenomena that are being studied are recorded?
  2. How are they depicted?
  3. What can we discover? Do you see any patterns? Trends? Outliers? Similarities? Differences?
  4. How do the two versions of the same theme compare and differ in terms of the DATA collected, the DEPICTION used and the DISCOVERY that we may make?

 
 
 
 

Dear Data : Part 2 - Your Project

We introduce three more tasks that will get you thinking about themes and data that you might collect as we participate in our own Dear Data Project in #CreativeCoding this term.

Please complete them, and the three Dear Data Project tasks, before the end of Week 01.

We want you to collect and visualize data over the term in similar fashion to the Dear Data Project.

We will show you how to use Processing to create a Dear Data 100 demo that explores some aspect of the world that is interesting to you, using code to engage with your data (and vice versa) and some creative thinking to express your data in ways that are meaningful to you.

The topic is down to you, as are the data you decide to collect, and the data visualization you create to depict them.

With luck, you will learn about yourself and some aspects of the world as you embark upon some discovery by depicting your data in interesting and innovative ways.

The task, to be completed by the end of term, with our support, is open ended, and broadly specified - all we ask is that ...

Over this term, as part of the #CreativeCoding activity you will ...

Create a Processing sketch
that depicts a data set that you have collected over time
that explores some aspect of the world
that is interesting to you
through at least 100 observations (data points)

Hopefully you will do so ...

in creative & expressive ways
that you are pleased with and
that helps you learn (a little) about yourself & the world around you

4. Choose Some Possible Topics

So, where to start?
You will need to do a little reading and some thinking.
But that's great because universities are fantastic places to read and think.
It's pretty much what they are set up for! (plus some listening, and some discussion).

The Topic and Data that you collect must be:

  • ORIGINAL - the topic should be original - use the creativity techniques that we have touched upon, like SCAMPER to truy to make it unique;
  • MEANINGFUL - the topic must be something that interests you and is perhaps important to you;
  • ANONYMOUS - the data must not reveal personal information through which you could be identified;
  • NUMEROUS - you must collect a minimum of 100 observations to show in your demo, which must show at least 100 data items visually;

So have a think.
What could you do?
What are you interested in?
What could you collect?
What might work for you?

Come up with lots of alternatives - things that excite or amuse you. There are no bad ideas at this stage, let the ideas flow.

Here you can see some guidance on how to start a Dear Data project from Giorgia & Stefanie. 👉

Read through ... but think about something that interests you other than podcasts.

Task 4 : Dear Data 100 (preliminary thinking)

Have a look at the guidance provided at the bottom of the Dear Data And FiveThirtyEight Want You To Visualize Your Podcast Habits page.

Begin to think about the data that you might want to collect - and see whether this is feasible.
Practice, try a few things out ...

  • is this interesting?
  • is it feasible?
  • does it work?
  • might it be ORIGINAL - MEANINGFUL - ANONYMOUS - NUMEROUS?

Then ask yourself the questions that Giorgia & Stephanie ask:

  • What do you want to know and explore? - What is your main question?
  • What detail and context do you need to record? - What are the additional questions?

Try to find two or three topics and think about which questions you could ask and what you could record. And how.
Discuss them with others - do they seem feasible? Interesting? Motivating?
Are they ORIGINAL - MEANINGFUL - ANONYMOUS and do they have the potential to be NUMEROUS?
